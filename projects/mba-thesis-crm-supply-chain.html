Assessing the Role of CRM Strategies in Enhancing Supply Chain Performance within India's Automotive Sector

Master's thesis for the MBA & Engineering programme (Automotive Management), submitted April 2025. Mixed-method research with an industry survey, eight expert interviews and a secondary-data review.

What this was

A thesis on how CRM strategies affect supply chain execution in the Indian automotive industry, drawing on three years of hands-on CRM work at Amazon and service and after-sales exposure from an internship at Tata Motors.

  • Ran an online survey of industry professionals and eight interviews with employees across manufacturing, distribution, after-sales and sales, from one to more than ten years of experience.
  • Added a secondary-data analysis of published research to test whether the primary findings held up.
  • Found that CRM's clearest effect is on inventory management, with gains in order fulfilment, supplier relationships and cross-department collaboration. Demand forecasting and customer satisfaction results were more mixed.
  • Identified the main barriers: limited customization, high maintenance cost, weak integration with ERP, and system complexity. Smaller suppliers often still manage customer data by hand.
  • Closed with seven prioritized recommendations, from unifying CRM with ERP and logistics platforms to AI and automation, training and change management, data governance and SME adoption.

    • Why it's here

    It connects the two halves of my background: years of hands-on CRM work, and an MBA and engineering view of how customer data moves through a supply chain.

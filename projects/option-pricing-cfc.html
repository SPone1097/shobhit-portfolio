Corporate Finance & Controlling — Option Pricing

An options-pricing analysis across four pharmaceutical companies, covering moneyness, time decay, implied volatility, and the Black-Scholes model.

What this was

A solo options-pricing analysis run across four pharmaceutical companies, working through the core mechanics of how options are priced and what moves that price.

  • Calculated moneyness (ITM/ATM/OTM) for calls across all four companies and analyzed the option chain for spread and liquidity patterns.
  • Charted time decay for each company, confirming the expected negative-exponential curve as expiry approached.
  • Tracked implied volatility over a five-week window, finding Eli Lilly consistently highest and JNJ consistently lowest.
  • Ran all four companies through a Black-Scholes calculator, then built out a Bear Call spread and a Married Put hedge for each.

    • Why it's here

    It's the clearest evidence of financial fluency on file — reading option chains, running a pricing model, and turning the output into an actual trading strategy.
